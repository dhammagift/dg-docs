# Uposatha days

<PageTools />

According to the suttas, Uposatha is kept on the 8th, 14th and 15th days of the lunar half-month
(MN 83). Below are the coming days as exact moments of the moon's phases — new moon, first
quarter, full moon, last quarter — converted to dates in your time zone.

<UposathaCalendar />

A day in the suttas is counted from the evening ("nights and days", AN 3.70), so the observance
begins on the evening before the date shown.

:::caution Dates can differ by a day
The moments come from an astronomical model, accurate to about a minute. Thai, Sri Lankan and
Burmese communities calculate their calendars by tradition and can differ from these dates by a
day; if you keep Uposatha with a particular community, follow its calendar. To cross-check, see
[Time and Date: Moon Phases](https://www.timeanddate.com/moon/phases/).
:::

## Key suttas to start with

**AN 10.46** — skipping the uposathas is not allowed ("it is your loss and failure").
<SuttaLink index={0}>Read →</SuttaLink>

**MN 83** — one should observe the 14th, 15th, and 8th days. <SuttaLink index={1}>Read →</SuttaLink>

**MN 146** — the 14th of the waxing moon, the 15th full moon. <SuttaLink index={2}>Read →</SuttaLink>

**AN 3.37** — the 14th, 15th, and 8th days compared with the visits of deities of different
ranks. <SuttaLink index={3}>Read →</SuttaLink>

**AN 3.70** — "nights and days" rather than "days and nights"; the Pali has the fixed expression
*ahoratta* ("day-night") in other contexts. The sutta also explains how Uposatha should **not** be
observed — the "cowherd's Uposatha" and the "Nigaṇṭha's Uposatha" — and how the Noble One's
Uposatha should be observed. <SuttaLink index={4}>Read →</SuttaLink>
