Voice / TTS

"There are these five dangers, monks, for one who recites the Dhamma in a drawn-out singing voice." — AN 5.209

Try it: the line below is already selected — press ▶ at the bottom of the frame and listen:

Open in a new window

5 ways to start the voice​

  1. Click the 🔉 icon next to the text's title: next to the Pali title it reads the Pali, next to a translation's title that translation.
  2. Click any Pali or translation sentence to highlight it, then press the ▶ button that appears at the bottom.
  3. Tap the ✦ before or after a line and choose Voice in its menu.
  4. Open a link to a line (for example /an5.209:1.1): the line is highlighted and ▶ is already there.
  5. Press Alt+R (Option+R on Mac).

On the search results page Alt+R reads the quotes found, in the same player: each sutta's name first, then its quotes, and the row being read is highlighted.

Player​

  • Controls — Play/Pause, previous/next sentence.
  • Navigation — pause, click a Pali sentence to highlight it, press Play to resume from there.
  • Mode — what is read: Pāḷi / Pāḷi + translation / translation / translation + Pāḷi.
  • Speed — the speed button opens a slider with presets; the default for Pāḷi is 0.7× ("normal" for the DG voice).
  • Memo — opens the Memo page.
  • AB — the A-B loop.
  • Settings (⚙) — Scroll (S: the text follows the voice), the pause between phrases in seconds, Pause only between paragraphs, and Voice settings.

Voices: three kinds​

⚙ → Voice settings has two rows, Pāḷi and Translation: tap a row, pick the kind (DG Voice, Google Cloud or System), then the voice; for Google, first its language. The ▶ next to a row plays a short sample (DG voices). ↺ brings back the default voices; downloaded voices stay. On the first play a tip lists the three kinds; × hides it.

The first neural voices that speak Pāḷi

DG Voice are the first neural voices that read Pāḷi as plain speech, not chant: with Pāḷi's long and short vowels, and without the singing voice AN 5.209 warns about.

DG Voice — our own free neural voices (default)​

They run on our server: no key, no limits, nothing to install. Each one can also be downloaded to your device and then reads without internet.

  • Pāḷi
    • Pratham ♂ · Piper (default): reads Pāḷi by our pronunciation rules.
    • Cūḷa DG ♂ · beta: trained on real Pāḷi readings.
    • Priyamvada ♀ · Piper: a female voice, the same pronunciation rules.
  • English: Yojanāni DG ♂ (US, default), Alan ♂ (UK), Kathleen ♀ (US).
  • Russian: Cūḷa DG ♂ (default), Puriso ♂, Irina ♀.
  • Pāḷi words inside a translation (sutta, Dhamma, bhikkhu, names) are read the Pāḷi way. Abbreviations are spelled out (Ven. → Venerable, SN 56.11 → Saṁyutta Nikāya 56, 11). Russian words get their stress from the context (дела́ его / иметь де́ла), online and with a downloaded voice alike, and ё where the text prints е.
  • The next sentences are prepared while one is playing, so there are no gaps between them.

Limitations​

  • Pāḷi: rare words and long compounds are sometimes pronounced inaccurately.
  • Pāḷi: a line of one or two words sounds less clear than the same words inside a phrase.
  • Cūḷa DG (Pāḷi): the sound is still rougher than Pratham's.
  • Russian: where the stress depends on the meaning (за́мок / замо́к), it is sometimes wrong.
  • Kathleen: low sound quality.

Offline: download a DG voice​

In Voice settings → DG Voice, each voice in the list has a ⬇ icon with its download size. Once downloaded, the voice reads on your device with no network, on the reader and on the Memo page. The 🗑 icon deletes it. The English and Russian voices download a shared pronunciation module with the first of them. While a DG voice is reading online, the player itself offers Listen offline · Download with the voice's size; × hides the offer.

Cūḷa DG and Yojanāni DG voices: all rights reserved

The Cūḷa DG and Yojanāni DG voices are made from recordings of real people's voices. All rights in these voices and their models are reserved.

Permitted only: downloading these voices and listening to texts with them on the Dhamma.Gift website and in the Dhamma.Gift apps.

Prohibited without prior written permission from Dhamma.Gift:

  • any use of these voices or their models outside the Dhamma.Gift website and apps, personal and non-commercial use included;
  • copying, distributing, publishing or passing on the model files to third parties;
  • cloning or imitating these voices;
  • training or fine-tuning other models on these models or on the audio they produce;
  • any commercial use.

This restriction is also stated in the file of each such model.

Google — cloud voices with a free quota​

High-quality cloud voices. Pāḷi is read by a Punjabi (pa-IN) voice through Devanagari with our own fixes. They work with a built-in trial key; for regular use enter your own Google API key (see below), whose free monthly quota is generous for personal use.

System — your device's voices, work offline​

Uses the speech synthesis of your system or browser: no internet needed, nothing is sent anywhere. There is no Pāḷi voice in any system, so install one of Sanskrit (India), Hindi (India), Nepali or Indonesian for the closest pronunciation; if none is installed, English is used as a fallback.

  • Android — Settings → Accessibility → Text-to-speech output, then install voice data for the language: Android help.
  • iPhone / iPad — Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices: Apple help.
  • Mac — System Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → System voice → Manage Voices: Apple help.
  • Windows — Settings → Time & language → Speech → Manage voices → Add voices: Microsoft help.

After installing a voice, reload the page and pick it in Voice settings → System.

A-B loop — repeat a passage for memorization​

The AB button next to the player is a separate tool layered on top of normal playback: pick a start and end point in the text, and the player repeats just that passage, with a pause between repeats and a repeat limit (or infinite).

  • Click AB — if a line was already selected, it immediately becomes point A and the panel waits for you to click a line for point B; if nothing was selected, click a line for A first, then for B. As soon as both points are set, the loop starts automatically.
  • The panel opens under the player; AB opens and closes it again.
  • Pause between loops — seconds to wait between repeats (− / + or type a number).
  • Repeats — defaults to ∞ (infinite); set your own number and the loop stops on its own once it's done. The 🗑 button clears both points.
  • To move a point, click it again and pick a new line. To clear a point, right-click (or long-press on phones) directly on the A or B button.
  • The passage between the two points is marked in the text with a vertical bar on the left.

Hotkeys​

KeyAction
Alt+RToggle Voice Mode
SToggle autoscroll
SpacePlay / Pause
← → or ↑ ↓Previous / Next segment
1Mode: Pāḷi
2Mode: Pāḷi + Translation
3Mode: Translation
4Mode: Translation + Pāḷi
+ / -Increase / decrease speed
RReset speed to default
Space and the arrow keys only work with auto-scroll on

If "Autoscroll" is off (the S key, or the toggle in the player settings), Space and the arrow keys stop responding to player control — that's expected behavior, not a bug. The 1–4 keys and +/- always work, regardless of autoscroll.

Panel footer​

  • Voice settings — kinds of voices, languages and voices, samples, the Google API key.
  • File — a link to an audio file, when one exists for that text.
  • Help ? — this page.

Google API key (optional)​

Only for the Google voices, when the trial key isn't enough. Create a project in Google Cloud, enable the Text-to-Speech API and create an API key (how to start); once a Google voice is chosen, Voice settings shows a key field under the two rows: paste it there. Free monthly limits and prices: Google Cloud pricing. By default translations use the cheaper Standard voices, Pāḷi a premium one.

Whole texts in MP3​

For some texts, an MP3: line under the text (for example MP3: Pali · English) gives the whole sutta as one file, in Pāḷi and in the translation, read by the DG voices; hover the translation link to see the translator. The Vinaya's two Vibhaṅgas have audio as well: Pāḷi for 347 rules and Russian for 34.

MP3 for the Memo page​

On the Memo page the ⬇ button saves the text as one MP3 in the voice you chose: with the DG voice the pauses between lines can be of any length (Google's are limited to 10 seconds).
